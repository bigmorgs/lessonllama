<p>On July 14, 2026, Anthropic launched Claude for Teachers — free premium AI access for verified K-12 educators in the US, grounded in a live database of academic standards across all 50 states. It connects with platforms like OpenSciEd and Illustrative Mathematics, includes FERPA-aligned data terms, and gives teachers access to Claude's full agentic capabilities at no cost.</p>
<p>It's a genuinely impressive launch. If you teach in the United States, it's worth signing up.</p>
<p>But if you're reading this, there's a reasonable chance you're not teaching in the United States.</p>
<h2>Who Claude for Teachers Is Built For</h2>
<p>The product's core value proposition is standards alignment. Learning Commons — the connector that grounds Claude's lesson planning — maps to academic standards in all 50 US states. The curricula it connects to are OpenSciEd and Illustrative Mathematics: excellent, research-backed resources used widely in American classrooms.</p>
<p>For a Year 8 MYP Design Technology teacher in Kuala Lumpur, a GCSE Business Studies teacher in Singapore, or an A-Level Biology teacher in Dubai, almost none of this applies. The standards database doesn't include IB MYP objectives. The connected curricula don't map to the Cambridge framework. The lesson planning prompts don't know what a Statement of Inquiry is, or why ATL skills matter, or how to align a summative task to MYP Criterion D.</p>
<p>This isn't a criticism of Claude for Teachers — it's doing exactly what it set out to do, and doing it well. It's simply not built for international school teachers.</p>
<h2>What General AI Tools Get Wrong About International Planning</h2>
<p>This is a problem teachers in IB World Schools have been navigating for years. General AI tools — ChatGPT, Gemini, and yes, standard Claude — are reasonably good at generating lesson content. Ask them to write a lesson on photosynthesis and you'll get something usable.</p>
<p>Ask them to write a Year 9 MYP Sciences unit planner, correctly aligned to Criterion B with a properly structured Statement of Inquiry connecting the Key Concept of Relationships to the Global Context of Scientific and Technical Innovation, and things start to fall apart quickly. The output looks plausible but misses the specific language, structure, and conceptual framing that IB planners require. You end up fixing more than you generated.</p>
<p>The same is true for IGCSE, where command words in mark schemes carry precise meaning. For A-Level, where assessment objectives map to specific skills. For the Australian Curriculum, where cross-curriculum priorities and general capabilities need to be explicitly addressed.</p>
<p>International and curriculum-specific planning has a layer of complexity that general AI tools weren't designed for. That's not a flaw — it's just scope.</p>
<h2>The Gap Claude for Teachers Confirms</h2>
<p>The Claude for Teachers launch is actually clarifying. It shows that when AI is built specifically for a curriculum context — grounded in real standards, connected to real resources, shaped around how teachers in that system actually plan — it becomes genuinely more useful than a general tool.</p>
<p>That's the gap Lesson Llama is being built to fill, for international school teachers.</p>
<p>Not a general AI assistant. Not a chatbot that generates lesson plans when you prompt it correctly. A planning tool that already understands the IB MYP framework, knows what criterion alignment looks like, speaks the language of the curriculum you actually teach — and builds the planning structure around you, not around a US state standard you've never heard of.</p>
<h2>What This Means Practically</h2>
<p>If you're a US K-12 teacher: sign up for Claude for Teachers. It's free, it's well-built, and the standards alignment is genuinely useful.</p>
<p>If you're an IB, IGCSE, A-Level, AP, or international curriculum teacher: Claude for Teachers wasn't built for you. General Claude can help with lesson content, and it's worth using. But for the specific structural and framework demands of international curriculum planning, you're still working around a tool that doesn't really understand your context.</p>
<p>That's the problem Lesson Llama is being built to solve.</p>
